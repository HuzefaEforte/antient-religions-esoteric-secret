# Lila — divine play

**Lila** ("play") is the Hindu answer to *why anything was created* that [[S0002]] uses to
dissolve the "intentional vs. automatic" dichotomy about creation (see [[emanation]]).

## The idea

[tradition] Brahman lacks nothing, so creation cannot be for a *goal* — it is **play**: free,
spontaneous, purposeless expression. [[S0002]]'s image: a child building a sandcastle — *"not
quite intentional (no goal beyond playing), not quite mechanical (nothing forces it)."* Play is a
**third thing** beyond the usual binary.

- Paired in [[S0002]] with the analogy of **love** and **laughter**: a mother never *decides* to
  love, yet nothing is more truly hers; nobody *decides* to laugh, yet laughter isn't mechanical.
  *"Love is exactly where 'on purpose' and 'by nature' become the same thing."*
- The mystics' cognate images: Eckhart's *ebullitio* ("boiling over"), Ibn ʿArabi's "Breath of the
  All-Merciful," the Rig Veda's "It breathed, windless, by its own impulse." See [[emanation]],
  [[meister-eckhart]].

## Why it matters

Resolves the al-Ghazali (intentional *kun fa-yakun*) vs. Avicenna (necessary overflow) debate not
by picking a side but by pointing to a category — *expression* — where will and nature coincide
(classical "divine simplicity"; Aquinas: God creates *freely* yet *without deliberation*). See
[[emanation]] and [[convergence-and-its-critics]].
