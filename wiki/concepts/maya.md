# Maya — cosmic illusion

**Maya** is the Hindu (especially [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]) principle that the world of
separate, solid objects is an appearance overlaid on the single underlying reality
([[atman-brahman|Brahman]]) — "the cosmic illusion that makes the one look like many" [[S0001]].

## The concept

[tradition] Maya is not "unreal" so much as **not-what-it-appears**: it presents multiplicity and
separation where there is actually unity. Mistaking the appearance for the whole truth is
**Avidya** (ignorance of one's true nature) — the Hindu counterpart the source pairs with the
Sufi **Ghaflah** (heedlessness). [[S0001]]

- In [[S0001]]'s **cinema-screen** mapping: Maya = the projected movie; Brahman = the screen;
  Avidya = believing the movie is 100% real. See [[the-observer]].
- Maya wraps the [[four-states-of-consciousness]]; waking through it (to **Turiya**) is
  realization (*Jnana*).

## Cross-tradition echoes (per S0001)

- Islamic **Dunya / Khalq** (the created, transient world) and **Ghaflah** (spiritual
  forgetfulness) are treated as parallels. `[esoteric]`/`[llm-generated]` — see
  [[islam-hindu-esoteric-parallels]].

## Corroboration & enrichment (S0002)

[[S0002]] gives Maya its classic image: **the rope mistaken for a snake in dim light** — "the world
isn't fake; you're *misreading* it" (Shankara's [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]). It adds **Kashmir
Shaivism**'s sweeter version: God is an actor so absorbed in playing "you" that he forgot he is the
actor — and waking (*pratyabhijna*, "recognition") is simply remembering (see [[remembrance]]).
