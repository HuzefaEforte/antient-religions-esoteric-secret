# The Two Truths (and Emptiness)

The **two truths** doctrine, formalized by **[[nagarjuna]]** (Madhyamaka Buddhism), is [[buddhism|Buddhism]]'s
structural parallel to esoteric shell/kernel — and, per [[S0002]], the tradition's **principled
dissent** from the "one God / one Self" framing of [[non-duality]].

## The doctrine

[tradition]
- **Conventional truth** (*samvriti*) — the everyday world of you, me, tables, chairs; real enough
  to live by.
- **Ultimate truth** (*paramartha*) — **shunyata** (emptiness): nothing exists *independently*.
  Not "nothing exists," but "nothing exists on its own." [[S0002]] keeps the same ocean image used
  elsewhere: "a wave looks separate, but it's just the ocean waving."
- **Buddha-nature** — already present in every being, "like the sun behind clouds": uncovered, not
  built. **Zen** dramatizes its unsayability (the Buddha silently holds up a flower; one student
  smiles — the transmission). See [[via-negativa]].

## The honest clash (the dissent)

[scholarly]/[tradition] Buddhism's **anatta** ("no permanent self") **directly negates** the Hindu
**[[atman-brahman|Atman]]** (the Self *is* the Absolute). [[S0002]] insists these are *"genuinely
rival final answers,"* not a translation difference — while noting both agree the little everyday
"me" is the illusion to see through. This is the key correction to [[S0001]]'s flattening of all
traditions into one doctrine; it is developed on [[convergence-and-its-critics]].

- Buddhism also **dissents on creation**: no creator, no cosmic purpose — things "arise depending
  on other things" (dependent origination) — yet it lands in the same practical place: *wake up,
  now*. See [[emanation]], [[lila]].
- The Buddha **refused** the origin question outright (the parable of the poisoned arrow). See
  [[axial-age]].
