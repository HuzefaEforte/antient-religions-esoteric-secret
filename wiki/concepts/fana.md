# Fana — annihilation of the self

**Fana** (Arabic: فناء, "passing away / annihilation") is the Sufi state in which the individual
ego dissolves, so that only the Divine remains. In [[S0001]] it is the experiential mechanism
behind [[wahdat-al-wujud]] and the deaths of [[al-hallaj]] and [[imam-hussain]].

## The concept

[tradition] The mystic strips away every personal preference, memory, and ambition ("treating
oneself as a corpse being washed") until no separate self is left to obstruct the Divine.
[[S0001]] gives the classic staged form: **Fana fi-Sheikh** (annihilation in the master) →
**Fana fi-Rasul** (in the Prophet) → **Fana fi-Allah** (in the Divine). Its complement is
**Baqa(t)** — "subsistence," eternal life *in* the Source after the ego is gone.

- Illustrated in [[S0001]] by the **iron in the fire** (iron glows and "becomes" fire without
  ceasing to be iron) and the **hollow flute** (empty of self, it sounds only the musician's
  breath) — the same images used to explain [[al-hallaj|"Ana al-Haqq"]].
- Paired with the Hindu **Neti-Neti** ("not this, not that") as a method of dis-identifying from
  body and thought — see [[the-observer]] and [[four-states-of-consciousness]].

## Fana and time

[esoteric] Because time is a property of the created world, [[S0001]] claims *Fana* also
collapses the sense of linear time, dropping the mystic into the timeless **Now** (Sufi *Waqt*,
Vedantic *Sat*). This underpins the source's reading of **sacrifice/martyrdom** as liberation
(the falcon leaving the cage) rather than tragedy — see [[imam-hussain]], [[al-hallaj]], [[rumi]].
