# Atman and Brahman

**Atman** is the true self / innermost consciousness; **Brahman** is the absolute, infinite
reality. The central teaching of **[[advaita-vedanta]]**, echoed throughout [[S0001]], is that
**they are identical** — the basis of [[non-duality]] in the Hindu tradition.

## The identity

[tradition] Because the witnessing consciousness (**[[four-states-of-consciousness|Turiya]]**) is
boundless and universal, the individual self *is* the cosmic Absolute. Expressed in the
*Mahavakyas* ("great sayings") the source quotes:
- **Aham Brahmasmi** — "I am the Absolute Reality."
- **Tat Tvam Asi** — "That Thou Art."

[tradition] **Nirguna Brahman** is the Absolute "without qualities" — the unknowable Essence,
which [[S0001]] treats as the Hindu counterpart of the Sufi **Dhat** (the unmanifest Divine
Essence beyond even the [[emanation|First Intellect]]'s grasp). See [[wahdat-al-wujud]].

## Analogies used

- **Ocean and wave / drop:** "You are not a drop in the ocean; you are the entire ocean in a
  drop" (quoted from [[rumi]]).
- **Gold** shaped into crown, map, and ring — one substance, many forms. See [[non-duality]].

## Tensions

- The strict identity of self and Absolute is the **Advaita** position specifically; other
  Vedanta schools qualify or deny it (see the note in [[non-duality]]). Not flagged by the source.
