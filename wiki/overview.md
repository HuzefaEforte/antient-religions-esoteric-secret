# Overview — Ancient Religions & Esoterica

Top-level orientation for the wiki. The maintainer revises this as sources accumulate and
the synthesis shifts.

## Scope
Ancient religions, mystery traditions, mysticism, and esoteric/occult knowledge — their
deities, figures, texts, places, symbols, rituals, and the doctrines and cosmologies that
connect them. A special interest of this domain is **syncretism**: how the same beings,
symbols, and ideas recur, merge, and transform across traditions.

## Current working thesis
The through-line the first source ([[S0001-ancient-religions-esoteric-secrets|S0001]]) chases is
that a single insight — **[[non-duality]]** (observer and observed are one) — is the shared
esoteric core beneath Islam, Hinduism, and Western esotericism. Islam's
**[[wahdat-al-wujud|Wahdat al-Wujud]]** and Hinduism's **[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]** are its two
faces; **[[emanation]]** (the Neoplatonic → Islamic Ten Intellects) supplies the cosmology, and the
Ismaili **[[drama-in-heaven]]** the myth of how the One became the many.

**Held with caution.** This thesis currently rests on a *single `llm-generated` chat*. The claimed
cross-tradition equivalences are that source's synthesis, not established scholarship, and its
practical "wealth / manifestation" layer is modern self-help dressed as ancient secret — both are
flagged in [[perennialism-and-modern-overlay]]. The thesis firms up (or gets challenged) as
`scholarly` and `primary` sources are ingested.

## Major traditions in play
- **[[sufism]]** (Islamic mysticism) · **[[tayyibi-ismailism]]** (Ismaili cosmology)
- **[[advaita-vedanta]]** (Hindu non-dualism) · the **[[upanishads]]**
- **[[hermeticism]]** (Western esoteric) · Neoplatonism (via **[[plotinus]]**)

*(Expected to grow — e.g. Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Gnostic, Orphic/mystery cults, Kabbalistic,
alchemical — as sources arrive.)*

## How to read this wiki
- Start at [[index]] for the full catalog.
- Entity and concept pages carry the substance; `synthesis/` pages carry comparisons and
  arguments; `sources/` pages record provenance and summaries.
- Claims are attributed by **reliability** (scholarly / primary / tradition / esoteric /
  speculative / llm-generated) — see `CLAUDE.md` §5. Read the voice, not just the claim.
