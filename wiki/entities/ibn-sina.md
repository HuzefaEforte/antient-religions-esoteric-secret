# Ibn Sina (Avicenna)

**Ibn Sina** (Avicenna, c. 980–1037 CE) was the most influential philosopher of the Islamic
Golden Age. In [[S0001]] he is the exponent of **emanation as automatic, necessary overflow** and
of the soul's "exile and return."

## Contribution (per S0001)

[scholarly]/[esoteric]
- **Necessary emanation:** Ibn Sina argued the **First Intellect** proceeds from God as an
  **automatic, eternal overflow** (*Fayd* / *Sudur*) — like light from the sun or heat from fire —
  not a deliberate, timed decision. He distinguished the **Necessary Existent** (God) from
  **contingent** beings whose existence must be sustained. See [[emanation]].
- **The "Drama in Heaven" / visionary recitals:** the source credits Ibn Sina's allegorical
  recitals (e.g. *Hayy ibn Yaqzan*) with the image of the **soul as a royal prince/bird** that
  descends, forgets its home, and must return — the template for [[fana|the return]] and the
  [[drama-in-heaven]].

## Tension
- Ibn Sina's "automatic overflow" was the exact point **Sufis and theologians rejected** as making
  God an impersonal machine, insisting instead on **will and love** (*Iradah*). [[S0001]] stages
  this philosophers-vs-mystics debate and then "reconciles" it (intent = process for the timeless
  Divine). See [[emanation]], [[wahdat-al-wujud]].
