# Advaita Vedanta

**Advaita Vedanta** ("non-dual" Vedanta) is the Hindu philosophical school teaching the identity of
the self and the Absolute — the primary Hindu tradition in [[S0001]] and its main partner to
[[sufism]].

## Core ideas (as they appear in S0001)

- **[[atman-brahman|Atman = Brahman]]** — the innermost self is the Absolute (*Aham Brahmasmi*,
  *Tat Tvam Asi*). The basis of [[non-duality]].
- **[[maya]]** — the world of separate objects is an illusion overlaying the one reality;
  ignorance of this is *Avidya*.
- **[[four-states-of-consciousness]]** — waking/dreaming/deep-sleep/**Turiya** (from the
  **[[upanishads|Mandukya Upanishad]]**); the true self is the witnessing Turiya.
- **Hidden knowledge:** *Gupta Vidya* — the inner teaching of the [[upanishads]] (Jnana Kanda),
  vs. ritual *Karma Kanda*. See [[tawil]].
- **Method:** *Neti-Neti* ("not this, not that") — see [[the-observer]].

## Cautions
- [scholarly] Advaita is **one** Vedanta school; **Vishishtadvaita** (qualified non-dualism,
  Ramanuja) and **Dvaita** (dualism, Madhva) dispute strict identity of self and God. [[S0001]]
  presents Advaita as *the* Hindu esoteric view without noting the alternatives.
