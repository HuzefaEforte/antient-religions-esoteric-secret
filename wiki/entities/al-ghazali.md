# Al-Ghazali

**Al-Ghazali** (c. 1058–1111), the enormously influential Persian theologian and Sufi, appears in
[[S0002]] as the champion of **creation as free, intentional act** against the philosophers'
necessary emanation.

## In S0002

[scholarly] In the classic dispute **al-Ghazali vs. the philosophers** (esp. **[[ibn-sina|Avicenna]]**):
- Avicenna: the world flows from God **necessarily**, like sunshine ([[emanation]]).
- Al-Ghazali: that turns God into a **machine**; God did not *need* to create but **chose** to —
  the Quran's *kun fa-yakun* ("Be! And it is"). *"A gift is only a gift if it could have been
  withheld."*

This is [[S0002]]'s "Camp 1" (completely intentional) in the intent-vs-overflow debate, later
transcended by the "third thing" — [[lila|play/love]]. See [[emanation]], [[convergence-and-its-critics]].
