# Ibn ʿArabi

**Ibn ʿArabi** (1165–1240 CE), the Andalusian Sufi metaphysician known as *al-Shaykh al-Akbar*
("the Greatest Master"), is credited in [[S0001]] with formulating **[[wahdat-al-wujud]]** (the
Unity of Being) — the Islamic articulation of [[non-duality]].

## Contribution (per S0001)

[tradition] Ibn ʿArabi is presented as the master of the **"Hidden Treasure"** metaphysics: the
universe is a **holographic mirror** in which the hidden Divine sees its own attributes, and the
perfected human being (*al-Insan al-Kamil*) is "the pupil of the cosmic eye." His work
***Fusus al-Hikam*** (*The Bezels of Wisdom*) is recommended in the source's reading list as the
key text on how creation lets the Divine know itself.

- Doctrine: nothing truly exists but the Real; creation = the Creator made visible. See
  [[wahdat-al-wujud]].
- His concept of the **Imaginal Realm** (*Alam al-Mithal*) — the intermediate "imaginal" world
  between matter and pure intellect — is invoked in the source (via Henry Corbin's *Alone with the
  Alone*) as the "software layer" where reality can be shaped. `[esoteric]`

## Syncretism / connections
- Placed alongside **[[advaita-vedanta]]** (Atman=Brahman) and **[[hermeticism]]** ("All is Mind")
  as expressions of one insight — [[S0001]]'s synthesis; see [[islam-hindu-esoteric-parallels]].

## Cautions
- [scholarly] *Wahdat al-Wujud* as a fixed doctrinal label was largely systematized by **later
  followers** (e.g. al-Qunawi, al-Qashani) rather than coined by Ibn ʿArabi himself, and it drew
  strong criticism within Islam. The source treats it as simply "his doctrine." See
  [[wahdat-al-wujud]].
