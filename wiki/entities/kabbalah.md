# Kabbalah

**Kabbalah** is the esoteric/mystical tradition of Judaism, introduced to this wiki by [[S0002]] as
the Jewish instance of the shell/kernel pattern — the inner ("*Sod*") layer of scripture.

## Core ideas (per S0002)

[tradition]
- **PaRDeS** ("orchard") — the four levels of reading scripture: **Peshat** (plain), **Remez**
  (allusive hint), **Derash** (interpretive lesson), **Sod** (*secret*). The Jewish parallel to
  [[tawil]]'s zahir/batin. The Talmud's **four sages who entered the "orchard"** (one died, one went
  mad, one lost faith, one emerged whole) is the tradition's own "DANGER: advanced material" sign.
- **Ein Sof** — God-in-Himself, "the Infinite beyond all names," pouring into manifestation through
  the ten **sefirot**. A Jewish [[emanation|emanation]] scheme; Ein Sof parallels the unknowable
  Essence (Sufi *Dhat* / [[atman-brahman|Nirguna Brahman]]).
- **Tzimtzum** (Isaac Luria, see [[isaac-luria]]) — the Infinite **contracted itself** to make room
  for a world ("a parent stepping back so a child can walk"); the vessels meant to hold the light
  **shattered**; divine **sparks** scattered into everything, awaiting **tikkun** (repair) — so
  every good deed can be cosmic. See [[lila]] ("love needs room"; "no king without a people").
- Traditionally **restricted** to trained, married men over forty — the guarding of the inner
  teaching (see [[esoteric-decoding]]).

## Connections
- The sefirot/Ein Sof structure is another **[[emanation]]** cosmology, comparable to the Ismaili
  Ten Intellects and Neoplatonic overflow.
- Key text: the **Zohar** (13th c.). Later figure: **[[isaac-luria]]** (Lurianic Kabbalah).
